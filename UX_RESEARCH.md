# ExpenseMargin UX direction

## Reference patterns

The v1.1 UX refresh borrows interaction and information-design patterns—not visual assets, copy, or proprietary code—from established financial operations products.

- Ramp: product-first storytelling and a simple see → understand → control narrative.
- Brex: concise hero, product UI shown immediately, proof/trust strip immediately after hero, action-oriented dashboard home.
- BILL: centralized view of urgent financial work and restrained navigation.
- Precoro: clear business-value positioning and predictable B2B plan presentation.

## ExpenseMargin design rules

1. Business software first, AI second. Avoid gradients, glowing cards, oversized pills, and AI-assistant visual tropes.
2. One primary CTA per surface. Secondary actions should look secondary.
3. Show real product concepts early: supplier, unit cost, percent change, annual impact.
4. Use flat white/neutral surfaces, restrained accent color, modest radii, and minimal shadows.
5. Keep the homepage story short: value → product proof → who it is for → three-step workflow → CTA.
6. Group application navigation by task so users scan rather than read a long flat menu.
7. Dashboard should answer three questions first: what changed, how much does it matter, and what needs attention.
8. Do not publish unverifiable customer logos or “trusted by” claims. Use truthful industry fit until customer proof exists, then replace the industry strip with verified logos and customer metrics.
